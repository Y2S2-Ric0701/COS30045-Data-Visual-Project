Where does it happen?

Question 2 showed injuries are rising. This page asks where: which states carry the most cases, whether living far from a city makes an injury more deadly, and whether every state follows the national trend.

Which states have the most injuries?

New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland: together almost 4 in every 5 cases. They are also the three states where most Australians live. (This sentence describes 2011–2021.)

Why it matters: it shows where hospitals carry the biggest load. It does not show where roads are most dangerous per person, because a big state has more cases simply because it has more people.

Is it more dangerous outside the cities?

Yes. Once in hospital, people from regional areas die about 25% more often than people from major cities (6.9 against 5.5 deaths per 1,000 cases). (This sentence describes all road users, 2011–2019.)

Why it matters: where you live affects your chance of surviving, not just of being hurt. Regional patients may face longer trips to a major trauma hospital. Question 5 looks at who is most likely to die.

The area is where the patient lives, not where the crash happened. Deaths are only recorded up to 2019, so this figure stops there. Cases with no recorded address are left out.

Is every state following the national trend?

No. Queensland rose the most of the big states (+65%), while New South Wales fell (−15%). Most of the NSW fall comes after 2017, when NSW stopped counting patients treated only in emergency. (This sentence describes 2011 to 2021.)

Why it matters: the national rise in Question 2 is not happening everywhere, and a counting change can look like a safety gain. Tasmania (+63%) and the Northern Territory (+56%) also rose fast, from small numbers.

What we learned

Most cases are where most people live (NSW, Victoria, Queensland), but a crash is more likely to be fatal for regional patients, and Queensland's numbers are rising fastest.
