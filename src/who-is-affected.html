Who ends up in hospital?

Before we ask whether road injuries are rising, or where and how they happen, we need to know who the patients are.

This page introduces the nine road-user groups. Every later page follows the same nine groups.

Which road users? Men or women? What age?
Figure 1.1 • Road users

Which road users are hurt most?

Car drivers, motorcyclists and cyclists make up about 3 in every 4 hospital cases. (This sentence describes all years, 2011–2021.)

Why it matters: these nine groups are the cast of the whole story. Click one to follow it through every chart on every page.

"Other or not recorded" includes car occupants whose seat (driver or passenger) was not recorded.

Figure 1.2 • Men and women

Are men and women hurt differently?

Two in three patients are men. Almost all injured motorcyclists (90%) and truck occupants (94%) are men, but most injured car and bus passengers are women. (This sentence describes all road users, 2011–2021.)

Why it matters: it shows who each safety message has to reach. A motorbike or truck campaign is mostly talking to men.

Each bar is the share of that group's patients who are men; the rest are women. By default it covers every year in the dataset (2011–2021); change the years in the filter bar. The dashed lines mark an even split (50%) and the average for all road users.

Figure 1.3 • Age

Which age is most at risk?

Young adults aged 17–25. The 40–64 group has the most cases only because it covers 25 years of age. For each year of age, 17–25 has nearly twice as many. (This sentence describes all road users, 2011–2021.)

Why it matters: the age groups are different sizes, so raw totals make the widest group look worst. Dividing by the number of years in each group gives a fair comparison.

Show:

"Per year of age" divides each group's cases by the number of ages it covers (17–25 covers 9 ages, 40–64 covers 25). The 75+ group has no upper age, so it can only be shown as a total.

What we learned

Most patients are car drivers, motorcyclists and cyclists; two in three are men; and young adults are hit hardest for their age.
