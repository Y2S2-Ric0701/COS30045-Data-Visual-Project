Who is hurt the worst?

So far we have counted injuries, but a broken wrist and a fatal head injury both count as one case. This page measures how bad the injuries are: who dies in hospital, and who stays longest.

Short answer: pedestrians, by a long way.

Who is most likely to die? Who stays longest? Biggest group = worst hurt?
Figure 5.1 • Dying in hospital

Who is most likely to die?

Pedestrians. About 22 in every 1,000 pedestrians admitted to hospital die there, around 4 times the rate for car drivers (6 per 1,000). (This sentence describes 2011–2019.)

Why it matters: a pedestrian has nothing between them and the vehicle, and Figure 4.2 showed 88% of them are hit by a car. Of all the road users, they are the most likely not to survive.

Why does this stop at 2019? The AIHW published this data in September 2023, but deaths after June 2020 had not yet been supplied by the states, so the death column is blank from July 2020 onward (blank, not zero). Including 2020–2021 would make every death rate look about a fifth lower than it really is. So every death and hospital-stay figure on this site uses 2011–2019, and both measures describe the same patients.
Figure 5.2 • Time in hospital

Who stays in hospital longest?

Pedestrians again: about 9 days on average, three times as long as cyclists (3 days). (This sentence describes 2011–2019.)

Why it matters: a longer stay means a more serious injury and a longer recovery. It is also the cost the hospital system carries.

Is the biggest group also the worst hurt?

No. Car drivers are the biggest group (33% of cases), and their share of deaths is about the same (32%). Pedestrians are only 7% of cases but 26% of deaths, while cyclists are 18% of cases but only 6% of deaths. (This sentence describes 2011–2019.)

Why it matters: this is the answer to the whole story. Counting who ends up in hospital (Question 1) is not the same as knowing who is hurt worst. Saving lives means focusing on pedestrians, even though they are a small group.

How to read it: for each road user, the grey bar is its share of all hospital cases and the dark blue bar is its share of all deaths in hospital. When the dark blue bar is longer, that group dies more often than its size would suggest. The number on the right says how many times more: 3.6× for pedestrians (in orange, because it stands out), 0.3× for cyclists.

What we learned

The road users we see most in hospital (drivers and cyclists) are not the ones hurt worst. Pedestrians are a small group, but they die most often and stay in hospital longest.
