What do people crash into most?

A car, ute or van (41% of cases). One in four (26%) involved no other vehicle at all: the person fell off or lost control. (This sentence describes all road users, 2011–2021.)

Why it matters: it shows which kinds of crash a safety measure would reach most often, and that many serious injuries need no second vehicle.

"Something else" is a motorbike, bicycle, pedestrian, animal, train or other non-motor vehicle. "Not recorded" means the hospital record does not say.

Is it different for each road user?

Yes, very. 88% of injured pedestrians were hit by a car, ute or van, while more than 40% of motorcyclist and cyclist injuries involved no other vehicle. (This sentence describes 2011–2021.)

Why it matters: each group needs a different fix. Protecting pedestrians means managing cars; protecting riders means road surfaces, skills and protective gear.

Each row is one road user and adds up to 100%. DarkerBrighter blue = a bigger share of that row; the outlined cell is what that road user hit most often.

Which crashes are deadliest?

Crashes with a truck or bus. They are only 3% of cases, but 20 in every 1,000 of those patients die in hospital, almost 3 times the rate after hitting a car (7 per 1,000). (This sentence describes all road users, 2011–2019.)

Why it matters: the most common crash (Figure 4.1) is not the most deadly. A heavier vehicle means a worse injury. Question 5 follows this through: who is hurt the worst?

Deaths in hospital per 1,000 hospitalised cases. Deaths are only recorded up to 2019, so this figure stops there. Darker blue = deadlier.

What we learned

Most crashes involve a car, and many involve no other vehicle. What you hit depends on who you are, and the heavier the other vehicle, the deadlier the crash.
