Hospitalised injuries from road crashes in Australia Every 13 minutes, someone is admitted to hospital after a road crash.

That is 108 people a day (2021). One question runs through this site: who gets hurt on Australian roads, and who gets hurt worst? Five short questions answer it, one page each.

Data: AIHW national hospital data • Injuries 2011–2021 • Deaths in hospital 2011–2019

Why this matters

Road deaths make the news. Road injuries fill the hospitals.

Far more people survive a road crash with a serious injury than die in one. In 2021, about 108 Australians a day were admitted to hospital after a crash on a public road, and that number is rising.

This site follows those patients, using the national hospital data published by the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW). Each question builds on the one before: first who, then whether it is getting worse, where and how it happens, and finally who is hurt the worst.

The story in one chart

Five questions, eight road users

Scroll on. The same eight bars answer each question in turn, and a different group comes out on top each time.

  1. Q1 Who is hurt the most?

    Car drivers are the biggest group: about 1 in 3 of the 410,000 people hospitalised after a road crash from 2011 to 2021. Motorcyclists and cyclists come next.

    Question 1: who is affected →

  2. Q2 Is it getting worse?

    Overall, yes: +16% from 2011 to 2021. But the groups split. Cyclists rose fastest, +51%, while pedestrians and car passengers fell.

    Question 2: trends over time →

  3. Q3 Where do they live?

    Each bar is the share of that group who live outside the major cities. The dashed line is all patients: 32%, about 1 in 3. For truck occupants it is more than half. Regional patients are also more likely to die once in hospital.

    Question 3: where it happens →

  4. Q4 What did they hit?

    Often nothing at all. More than half of injured cyclists, and half of motorcyclists, were hurt with no other vehicle involved. Bus occupants rank highest of all, at 69%.

    Question 4: crash factors →

  5. Q5 Who is hurt worst?

    Pedestrians. For every 1,000 pedestrians admitted to hospital, about 22 die there (21.8, or 2.2%). That is a rate, not a count, which is why it can have a decimal. It is more than 3 times the average of 6 per 1,000. The biggest group, car drivers, is close to the average: the biggest group is not the worst hurt.

    Question 5: injury severity →

Summary & what should change

The biggest group is not the worst hurt

The five questions in one paragraph: most patients are car drivers, motorcyclists and cyclists (Q1); the total is rising, led by cyclists (Q2); most cases are in the three biggest states, but regional patients die more often (Q3); most crashes involve a car, but trucks and buses are the deadliest (Q4); and pedestrians, only 7% of cases, account for a quarter of deaths in hospital (Q5).

Two findings stand out for road safety policy:

Vulnerable road users
4×

Once in hospital, pedestrians are about 4 times more likely to die than car drivers.

→ Policy: safer crossings and lower speeds where people walk.

Regional areas
+25%

People injured in regional areas have a 25% higher death rate in hospital than people in major cities.

→ Policy: faster transfer to trauma centres outside the cities.
