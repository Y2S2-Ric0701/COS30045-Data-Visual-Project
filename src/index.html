Hospitalised Injuries from Road Crashes in Australia

One question runs through this site: who gets hurt on Australian roads, and who gets hurt worst? Five short questions answer it, one page each.

Why this matters

Road deaths make the news. Road injuries fill the hospitals.

Far more people survive a road crash with a serious injury than die in one. In 2021, about 108 Australians a day were admitted to hospital after a crash on a public road, and that number is rising.

This site follows those patients, using the national hospital data published by the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW). Each question builds on the one before: first who, then whether it is getting worse, where and how it happens, and finally who is hurt the worst.

Summary & what should change

The biggest group is not the worst hurt

The five questions in one paragraph: most patients are car drivers, motorcyclists and cyclists (Q1); the total is rising, led by cyclists (Q2); most cases are in the three biggest states, but regional patients die more often (Q3); most crashes involve a car, but trucks and buses are the deadliest (Q4); and pedestrians, only 7% of cases, account for a quarter of deaths in hospital (Q5).

Two findings stand out for road safety policy:

Vulnerable road users
4×

Once in hospital, pedestrians are about 4 times more likely to die than car drivers.

→ Policy: safer crossings and lower speeds where people walk.

Regional areas
+25%

People injured in regional areas have a 25% higher death rate in hospital than people in major cities.

→ Policy: faster transfer to trauma centres outside the cities.
