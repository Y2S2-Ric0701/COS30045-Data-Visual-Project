Question 2 of 5

Is it getting better or worse?

Question 1 showed who ends up in hospital. This page asks whether that number rose or fell between 2011 and 2021, and for which road users.

Short answer: worse overall, mostly because of cyclists.

Did the total go up? Which groups rose or fell? Who changed most?
Figure 2.1 • The total

Did the total go up?

Yes. Hospital cases rose from 34,033 in 2011 to 39,505 in 2021, up 16%. (This sentence describes all road users, 2011–2021.)

Why it matters: Australia's road safety strategy aims to cut serious injuries, so a rising total means that goal is not being met. The coloured layers show which road users the extra cases came from.

The black line is the total for all road users. The dip in 2020 coincides with the COVID-19 lockdowns, when people travelled less.

Figure 2.2 • Each road user

Which groups are rising, and which are falling?

Cyclists rose the most (+51%), then car drivers (+22%). Pedestrians fell (−15%). (This sentence describes 2011–2021.)

Why it matters: one total can hide groups moving in opposite directions. The label at the end of each line gives its name and its change over the selected years, so you can read the answer without a legend. The dashed lines mark two changes in how hospitals counted patients; a dip right after one is partly a counting change, not fewer crashes.

Clicking a chip under the chart also follows that road user. Buses, trucks, and utes or vans have only a few hundred cases a year, so their lines sit near the bottom.

Figure 2.3 • Change ranked

Who changed the most?

Cyclists (+51%) and ute or van occupants (+38%) grew fastest. Bus occupants (−21%), pedestrians and truck occupants (both −15%) fell. (This sentence describes 2011 to 2021.)

Why it matters: this is Figure 2.2 boiled down to one number per group, so the winners and losers are easy to rank. Orange = more injuries (worse), green = fewer (better).

Change from the first to the last year chosen in the filter bar. Small groups have few cases, so a change of a few dozen patients shows up as a large percentage.

What we learned

Road injuries are going up (+16%), and the groups behind the rise are cyclists and car drivers. Pedestrian injuries are falling.
